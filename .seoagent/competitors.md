# Competitors — myframely.com

## Competitor 1: PlateMonitor — platemonitor.com
**Domain authority signal:** Ranked in the top 3-5 for 6+ of our research queries (cost-by-state, rules-by-state, character-limit).
**Content focus:** Programmatic doorway pages per state × page-type (`/states/ohio/rules`, `/states/illinois/cost`, `/states/north-dakota`). Covers all 50 states.
**Coverage gaps:** Pages read as templated/thin — rules + cost + a checker widget, little narrative or original insight. No product/commerce angle at all (pure utility). No content for gift-buyers.
**Style:** Table-heavy, minimal prose, clearly built at scale rather than per-state depth.

## Competitor 2: PlateHog — platehog.com
**Domain authority signal:** Directly overlaps on West Virginia (a state we both cover live). Already runs a blog with state-guide content ("2026 State-by-State Guide", "custom license plate cost").
**Content focus:** State pages + blog content + a live checker — the closest analog to what myframely should build.
**Coverage gaps:** Only 11 states covered. Maine, Kentucky, Wyoming, North Dakota are essentially open territory where PlateHog isn't competing at all.
**Style:** Blog-format guides, not pure doorway pages — more comparable quality to what we'd aim for.

## Competitor 3: PlateRadar — plateradar.com
**Domain authority signal:** 43-state coverage, appeared for "vanity plate availability checker" and branded searches.
**Content focus:** Freemium tool (300k+ tracked combos, $9/mo alerts), positions on data freshness ("last checked" timestamps).
**Coverage gaps:** Pure utility — no blog/content layer surfaced in search at all. Zero top-of-funnel content strategy.
**Style:** N/A (no content to speak of).

## Competitor 4: GoodCar — goodcar.com/vanity-plate-check
**Domain authority signal:** Ranks on generic "checker" / "is it taken" queries.
**Content focus:** Plate-checking is a minor feature bundled inside a much larger general car-services site.
**Coverage gaps:** Little dedicated content; the checker isn't the site's focus, so depth is shallow.

## Competitor 5: bestlicenseplateframes.com
**Domain authority signal:** Ranks for frame-idea and frame-pros-and-cons queries — the direct content competitor on the *frame* side (not the plate-checking side).
**Content focus:** Affiliate/content blog about license plate frames generally.
**Coverage gaps:** Generic, broad idea listicles — not tied to a specific product line, no checker tool, no gift-occasion niching.

## Competitor 6: Etsy (decoforge3d and the broader marketplace)
**Domain authority signal:** Dominates nearly every frame-shopping query (cute/funny/3D-printed/girly-car-accessories/gift-for-her-or-him).
**Content focus:** N/A — this is a **product competitor**, not a content competitor. Relevant for positioning/pricing awareness, not for the content strategy itself.
**Coverage gaps:** N/A.

## Note on state-rules content — the real official sources
For every one of our 8 live states, the actual state DMV/.gov page appears in results (e.g. ksrevenue.gov, WV Code, ME SOS, drive.ky.gov). These are not realistic to outrank on the exact rules text — the play is to be the friendlier, more complete, tool-backed version that links to (and cites) the official source rather than trying to beat it on raw authority.
